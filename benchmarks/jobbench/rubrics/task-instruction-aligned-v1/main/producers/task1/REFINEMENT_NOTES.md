## Likely judge artifacts versus genuine task failures

### Likely judge artifacts

- Suggested filenames were sometimes treated informally as expectations even though the instructions allow other filenames.
- The original weekend row did not acknowledge the handbook's separate straight-six option. A valid straight-six schedule should pass when its prerequisites are actually established.
- A memo should not fail for wording differences such as “standard” versus “normal” priority when the strict source rule and result are clear.

### Genuine task failures

- The refined Luna straight-six treatment was unsupported because the schedule did not establish all prerequisites, including the required weeknight pattern and announcement timing.
- Several schedules exceeded weekly hours, ignored the choreographer or piano constraints, or used unavailable rooms.
- Some forecasts did not begin with the source actuals or failed to flag each negative cumulative week.
- Some memos omitted production-team emergency-contact gaps, script/backorder reconciliation, or a complete closing action table.
- Some contact workbooks invented missing emergency contacts rather than flagging them for follow-up.
